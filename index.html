02Website fixes

Your ad gets the click. Your page decides the sale.

When sales slow, the ads get the blame. Often the page is the leak. I shop your site on a phone, the way someone fresh off an Instagram ad does, and write down everything that made me hesitate.

Agencies call this CRO, conversion rate optimisation. I call it finding what stops the sale. Every change comes as a before and after picture with one plain line on why it sells more. No jargon. No 40-page deck.

Three real stores, three fixes each. Drag the line to compare before and after.

OLIPOP, prebiotic soda, US. Checked on their live site, 7 Nov 2026.

1Tell one-pack buyers that 2 packs ship free

One 12 pack is $35.99 (about Rs 3,000). It sits under the $50 free-shipping line, so it pays $3.99 shipping (about Rs 330). Say so on the product page and offer the second pack right there. More people take two.

2Put calories and sugar on the first screen

A shopper from an ad came for one answer: why this beats regular soda. The answer, 50 calories and 5g sugar, sits three screens down. Put it under the price.

3Put the 45-day guarantee next to Add to Cart

Twelve cans of a flavour you have never tried feels like a gamble. OLIPOP already promises a 45-day guarantee, but only in the cart and the footer. Say it where the doubt is.

3 changes from a 7 Nov 2026 check.

Bummer, underwear and socks, India. Checked on their live site, 2 Nov 2026.

1Under the price, sell the second pair, not EMI

Nobody needs EMI (paying in monthly instalments) on a Rs 599 pair (about $7). The spot under the price is the first thing people read after the price. Use it for the offer that makes the order bigger.

2Say how it fits, right at the size buttons

"Will it fit?" is the big worry with underwear online. Their 1,296 reviews already answer it, four screens down. Say it at the size buttons, where people pick.

3Say "sold out" on the card, and collect a restock alert

Every boxer was sold out, yet the page looked open for business. Say so, and offer a WhatsApp ping when sizes return. A dead end becomes a lead.

3 of the 9 changes in the full report.

Sarkar, perfume, India. Checked on their live site, 2 Nov 2026.

1Make the checkout price the big price

People decide on the biggest number they see. Rs 2,199 (about $26) was big. The real checkout price, Rs 1,999 (about $24), sat in tiny text at the bottom. Swap them.

2Say what it is and what the deal is, on the first screen

The home banner had no product, no deal and no button. Someone arriving from an ad needs all three at once. One line and a button turn the banner into a shop window.

3Show the star rating under the name

998 people had rated this perfume, but the stars sat six screens down. Beside the name, they build trust before the shopper even reaches the price.
